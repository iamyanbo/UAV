# Adaptive Public-Market Mechanism Research

Discover, implement, and test defensible mechanisms for systematic decisions in
US public markets. The investable scope includes equities, ETFs, prospective
options observations, filings, macro series, and timestamped public news or
social evidence. This is research and prospective shadow observation only; it
does not place live orders.

Choose each next study by expected information value. Compare continuing the
current mechanism, testing a different explanation, obtaining missing evidence,
and stopping. There is no required number of candidates, components, or tasks,
and no forced breadth or depth cadence. Novelty is useful only when it produces a
testable difference; known methods with a defensible data, horizon, execution,
cost, or risk-management edge are equally valid.

Acquire data after a question makes it useful; do not build a universal market
archive in advance. Use the source monitor as a nonblocking inbox and persist
news as timestamped data only when it becomes experimental evidence. Public
social posts, forums, blogs, code, transcripts, filings, releases, journalism,
and academic work are all in scope; source type affects corroboration and
weight, not whether an idea may be investigated.

Empirical claims must respect what was observable at the decision time and
retain snapshot provenance. The lead and workers choose the evaluation,
comparisons, falsifiers, leakage controls, and reporting detail that the actual
claim requires. Preserve bounded, negative, and refuted findings instead of
optimizing one retrospective score.

Historical results remain retrospective. A candidate is not deployable until a
fixed, hashed implementation accumulates prospective shadow evidence across
meaningfully different conditions.
