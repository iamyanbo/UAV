Read this before using the idea shortlist

correction
The earlier “Gaussian + language + UAV” and “uncertainty-aware JEPA” gaps were too broad. SINGER, GRAD-NAV++, PiJEPA, Calibrated Predictive Safety, and Think Like a Pilot cover important parts already.

The remaining candidate is narrower: action-sensitive geometric prediction for 6-DoF flight, calibrated uncertainty that changes behavior, measured delay in meters of travel, and controlled transfer to held-out real routes.

1 · Glossary

VLM — vision-language model

role
reads one image plus a text prompt, answers in words.
note
a perceiver here: a later, smaller net turns its words into the flight command.

VLA — vision-language-action model

role
one network, image plus instruction in, the flight action out; no interface in between.
note
the dominant pattern in 2025–2026 work.

World model — three flavours

flavour a
Dreamer / RSSM. Compact recurrent latent state; imagined roll-outs inside the training loop; smallest compute per step.
flavour b
Generative video. Predicts the next frames in pixel or video-embedding space; richest signal; costliest step.
flavour c
JEPA. Predicts the embedding of the future, no pixel decoding; one distance in latent space plus a regulariser against collapse.
note
the three have begun to merge: PiJEPA covers language-conditioned latent planning, while Gaussian-JEPA and 4DGS-WAM cover predictive Gaussian representations. The remaining question is the full aerial, action-sensitive, uncertainty-aware, low-latency system.

3DGS / 4DGS — the geometry

3DGS
the scene as coloured anisotropic Gaussians; each primitive is a list of numbers — metric mean, log-scale spread, orientation, colour, opacity; renders fast and photorealistically.
4DGS
the same, time-indexed, splitting static background from movers, so only the movers need extrapolating.
opacity
already a per-primitive confidence, which scalar depth cannot express — the seed of the confidence-gate idea.

Prefill — the number that explains the rest

share
about 94.4% of on-device step time is the phase that reads the input, not the phase that writes the answer.
meaning
the input token budget binds first, parameter count second; one primitive set of a few hundred numbers is exactly such a budget.

2 · Anchor papers, fully annotated

VLM-Nav

PLOS ONE · Apr 2026
what
A three-stage, map-free pipeline for monocular aerial navigation, built against the two failure modes the authors flag: weak multimodal fusion and uninterpretable outputs.
how
  • Stage 1 — one RGB camera; monocular depth from DepthAnything-V2.
  • Stage 2 — image, depth map and a structured prompt go to a cloud VLM for obstacle and goal understanding.
  • Stage 3 — a small fusion network combines that answer with distance-sensor readings and picks one of 5 flight actions.
pattern
VLM-as-perception: the language model never emits control; a hand-designed decoder bridges to a classical controller.
results
Completion 0.98 on both AirSim scenes (Blocks, Downtown West); code released.
says
monocular depth plus a cloud VLM suffices for structured scenes; the compact fusion head keeps the loop real-time.
good
  • every stage testable in isolation
  • the 5-way action set makes the interface concrete
  • open code, two public scenes
bad
  • cloud round-trips inside the loop
  • only two synthetic scenes
  • monocular scale drift passes straight into stage 3

FlightGPT

2505.12835EMNLP 2025
what
A VLM-based aerial VLN framework with an explicit two-stage recipe, aimed at the three weaknesses the authors list: insufficient multimodal fusion, weak generalisation, poor interpretability.
how
  • Stage 1, SFT — supervised fine-tuning on high-quality demonstrations, to improve the initialisation and the structured reasoning before any reinforcement.
  • Stage 2, GRPO — group-relative policy optimisation under a composite reward: goal accuracy, reasoning quality, format compliance.
  • Reasoning — a chain-of-thought trace precedes the action, which is what makes the decision readable.
why it works
the composite reward is the load-bearing part: format-only training yields correct-looking but useless coordinates; the reasoning term forces the trace to agree with the emitted waypoint.
results
CityNav, leading every scenario; +9.22% over the strongest baseline on unseen environments.
says
reasoning-before-acting buys the unseen margin; the stage order matters because the first stage already carries structured reasoning.
good
  • interpretable traces, not only scores
  • clean, reproducible recipe
  • largest reported unseen-split margin among the anchors
  • open code
bad
  • no timing column at all
  • trace length is the latency budget — a long chain is a slow step
  • authors note no explicit staged/hierarchical planning inside the model

AirDreamer

2606.03252Jun 2026
what
A generalist navigator: a Dreamer-style world model for environment understanding with an RL policy on top, trained on imagined roll-outs — motivated by animal navigation, against hand-designed chains that generalise poorly across layouts.
how
  • State — compact recurrent latent; imagination replaces full pixel decoding per step.
  • Reward — deliberately sparse, no hand-crafted shaping, credited with escaping local-minimum traps and with useful yaw behaviour.
  • Transfer — effective sim-to-real with no extra tuning at deployment.
results
+5.3% over the best baseline in challenging maps; real flight to 1.8 m/s; scanning emerges without a shaping term.
says
for layout-sensitive navigation the imagination is the source of generalisation, and one sparse signal suffices to train it.
good
  • the cleanest zero-tuning transfer claim in the set
  • minimal reward engineering
  • emergent scanning shows the imagined state is being used
bad
  • no language channel
  • modest absolute speed, 1.8 m/s
  • gains are percentage-point scale

WorldFly

2606.06147Jun 2026
what
A world-model-based VLA where one coupled flow-matching head jointly predicts the next near-term observation and the next action, targeted at occlusion-heavy urban canyons with abrupt view change.
how
  • Two branches, one head — branch one completes the visual future, branch two the action; trained so the imagined frame conditions the action.
  • Why coupled — both outputs share one representation, so no separate planner is needed.
  • Extra — introduces the Urban-Canyon Traversal benchmark.
results

success, unseen31%16%10%

says
explicit future prediction helps most where the view changes abruptly; the authors price it as the dominant compute cost.
good
  • best-in-class on both splits
  • an elegant single-artefact joint head
  • useful new benchmark
bad
  • author-stated expensive frame prediction
  • seen→unseen halves (87→31), so generalisation binds

Qwen-Drive

2609.00111
what
A foundation-style VLM for ground driving: Qwen3.5-4B language core, bird's-eye-view perception, flow-matching trajectory planner.
says
competitive for its size; cite as a VLM-for-mobility exemplar; numbers are not transferable to a quadrotor since camera geometry and action space differ.
good
  • compact core plus flow-matching head is a good recipe
  • BEV is genuinely useful where poses are known
bad
  • not aerial
  • the BEV grid does not map cleanly onto a banked craft with a rolling view

CoDrone / CODrone

2504.20032
what
An oriented object-detection benchmark: 10,000+ high-resolution aerial photographs, 590,000+ labelled instances, twelve categories, oriented boxes.
says
a strong perception resource; there is no navigation paper under this name — a genuine dead end for the navigation question; a hobby product shares the name.
good
  • oriented boxes fit aerial viewpoints
  • usable as a perception pretraining source
bad
  • detection only: no instructions, no trajectories
  • cannot carry a navigation result alone

3 · The three architecture patterns

Pattern 3 is the 2025–2026 default; 1 and 2 survive where timing must be certified or the inner loop must be provably stable.

4 · Benchmarks and data

AerialVLN

2308.06735
what
Founded aerial VLN: 25 synthetic city scenes in UE4 with an AirSim front-end; over 25,000 written instructions; a discretised action space.
good
cheap, reproducible, still the reference split; discretised actions make tables comparable.
bad
synthetic only; coarse action space; its 20 m success radius is looser than 15 m elsewhere.

CityNav

2406.14240
what
First large real-world aerial set: 32,637 recorded paths over 4.65 km² of two real cities, rebuilt from laser-scanned point clouds; two interaction modes.
finding
every tested method improves once the geo-reference map is supplied — the ablation behind the 46.8%→22.5% figure.
good
truly real geometry, metric scale, a ready map ablation.
bad
two cities only; long paths stress memory; the map input is itself a confound.

OpenFly

2502.18041
what
One platform with four renderers at once: UE, a game engine, satellite tiles, and 3DGS from real photos; ≈100,000 paths over 18 scenes; an agent on a compact backbone.
results
OpenFly-Agent 26.09% on real outdoor flights; the 3DGS lane gives the best fidelity per unit cost.
good
the four-way renderer ablation is exactly what a representation study needs.
bad
renderer-to-renderer shifts leak into cross-paper tables; one instruction per path.

OpenUAV

2410.07087
what
Realistic platform and benchmark: multi-view camera streams plus the UAV-Need-Help set for open-world object search.
good
real scans, multi-view, open-ended goals; a consistent multi-view gain.
bad
instruction style differs from AerialVLN, so cross-table numbers are only approximate.

UAV-ON

2508.00288
what
Open-world object-goal benchmark: captions from a precomputing VLM form the semantic field; a separate agent then navigates.
good
clean two-stage protocol; captions cheap to regenerate.
bad
information lost at the caption interface; a fresh call per step.

MapNav

2502.13451
what
An annotated semantic map as the memory — an explicit queryable list of what has been seen — feeding a decoupled planner.
good
directly comparable to a primitive-indexed memory; easy to ablate.
bad
ground-robot origin; annotation quality is the ceiling.

AeroVerse

2408.15511
what
The benchmark suite around the aerial world model; it integrates over ten 2D/3D vision-language models, two pre-training sets, five fine-tuning sets, ten-plus metrics and a simulator.
data
  • AerialAgent-Ego10k — first-person real urban drone images, the first large real-world image-text pre-training set
  • CyberAgent-Ego500k — virtual image–text–pose aligned set for world-model pre-training
  • five task families: scene awareness, spatial reasoning, navigational exploration, task planning, motion decision
  • five matching instruction sets, 3k each
scoring
SkyAgentEval, a GPT-judged metric layer — it reveals where 2D versus 3D models differ on UAV-agent tasks.
good
  • the five-way split is the field's standard
  • aligned pose data is rare and directly useful
  • the scorer is scriptable
bad
  • judge noise needs a human-checked anchor subset
  • the five-way split is coarse
  • a per-step judge cost

  • UAV-VLN 2504.21432 — end-to-end with lightweight fusion.
  • UAV-VLRR 2503.02465 — rapid-response search-and-rescue split.

5 · VLM / VLA methods

SINGER — onboard generalist

2509.18610Sep 2025
what
Language-guided aerial navigation with onboard sensing and compute only, aimed squarely at the field's three complaints: scarce demonstrations, real-time control demands, unreliable external pose.
how
  • (i) Simulator — a photorealistic, language-embedded flight simulator using Gaussian Splatting, built to keep the sim-to-real gap small and data generation cheap.
  • (ii) Expert — an RRT-inspired multi-trajectory generator producing collision-free demonstrations.
  • (iii) Policy — a lightweight end-to-end visuomotor net for real-time closed-loop control, trained on ≈700k–1M observation–action pairs.
results
Over a velocity-controlled semantic guidance baseline: reaches the query +23.33% more often, holds it in view +16.67% more, 10% fewer collisions; superior zero-shot transfer to unseen scenes and unseen goal objects.
good
  • the whole chain inside one loop
  • the RRT expert makes labels cheap
  • only onboard hardware needed
bad
  • a fitted map per site
  • the expert's quality is the ceiling of the distilled policy

GRaD-Nav++ — VLM, Gaussian field, differentiable dynamics

2506.14009RA-L 11(2)
what
A lightweight VLA running fully onboard that follows natural-language commands in real time, built against hand-crafted skills, heavy tuning, and models too slow for the craft.
how
  • Simulator — a photorealistic 3DGS scene, so the fitted field is also the renderer.
  • Training — differentiable RL: gradients flow through renderer and dynamics together.
  • Head — a mixture-of-experts action head routing computation adaptively, improving generalisation while mitigating forgetting.
results
settingtrained tasksunseen tasks
simulation83%75%
real hardware67%50%
multi-environment average 81% sim, 67% real.
says
compact, efficient models suffice for reliable language-guided flight without external infrastructure; the MoE head is the documented anti-forgetting device.
good
  • the only entry with a full sim-and-real matrix on both splits
  • gradients through the renderer is a real efficiency gain
bad
  • a fitted scene per environment
  • several experts raise peak memory

SkyVLN

2507.06564
what
Planner–controller split: the language model emits sparse waypoints, then nonlinear MPC tracks them with the model inside its loop, in urban settings.
good
two speeds for free; a certifiable inner loop.
bad
waypoint coarseness limits curvature; no timing column.

AerialVLA

2603.14363
what
Minimalist end-to-end: a compact backbone with a small regression head emitting continuous references, no separate controller.
good
one artefact; clean continuous outputs.
bad
a full-depth cost every step; possible semantic fading after tuning.

VLA-AN

2512.15258
what
Onboard framework for complex environments: 3DGS-generated demonstrations, compact backbone, a cached prefix to stay warm between steps.
good
prefix caching is the standard cheap trick; runs on Jetson-class hardware.
bad
data cost moves into the map build; prefix staleness.

The 2026 method line

entries
  • AutoFly 2602.09657 — wild-oriented VLA, reduced instruction set, compact action tokeniser; the token vocabulary caps precision.
  • AeroDuo 2508.15232 — dual-stream, deliberative plus reactive fused per step; gains on long-horizon splits; two heads to calibrate.
  • OpenVLN 2511.06182 — open-world expansion with a lightweight reasoner; leads that split; nomadic goals stress every reasoner.
  • LookasideVLN 2604.17190 — a lookaside branch carries the heading into fusion, over memory graphs with lookahead; cheap and effective, one extra branch.
  • See-and-Reach 2606.20045 — decouples far-range discovery from in-field approach, the staged structure single-stage recipes miss; needs a stage boundary.
  • DynFly 2606.31654 — dynamic-aware continuous trajectory generation rather than sparse waypoints; smoother feasible tracks; chunk length must match the rate.
  • CognitiveDrone 2503.01378 — real-time cognitive solving and reasoning on the craft, reasoning layer around ≈2 Hz.

6 · World models — two families

Family A — compressed-state prediction

MAD
2606.04534 mapping-aware model whose core predicts the occupancy / visibility map rather than raw pixels — the lowest-dimensional useful state in the set. 9.66 m/s sim, 5.05 m/s real, forest, depth camera only. Good: cheapest state, few samples. Bad: texture and colour detail are lost.
Dream to Fly
2501.14377 model-based RL from images only, compact recurrent state, on a racing set: up to 9 m/s; the craft learns to point at texture-rich gates untaught. Sample efficiency and generalisation both flagged for the base recipe.
Dreaming Falcon
2511.18243 physics-informed model-based RL; better convergence with fewer samples against the plain recipe and two other model-based methods; the same latent family underneath.
SkyJEPA
2606.23444 latent-only prediction, no pixel decoding; trained in a flightmare-style simulator, deployed zero-shot with no extra real-world fine-tuning. Cheapest imagination; no language channel.
AirDreamer
see §2 — the same family with a deliberately sparse reward.

Family B — generative video plus language

WorldVLN
  • what 2605.15964 — first autoregressive world-action model for aerial VLN; short-horizon state transitions decoded to waypoint actions.
  • how a latent autoregressive video backbone; each executed segment's new observations are re-encoded into the same context, so prediction is closed-loop rather than one clip.
  • training two stages: ground the video prior in instruction-conditioned dynamics, then an action-aware group-relative update scoring waypoints by their rollout consequences.
  • results +12% over VLA baselines, larger on hard cases; zero-shot to a real craft.
  • bad autoregressive length is the budget; stage two may fade the language part.
AeroAct
  • what 2607.14997 — action-centred world-action model; the paper claims the first WAM instantiated and demonstrated for real-world aerial flight. Treat this as the authors’ scope claim, not evidence that the broader aerial VLA field is empty.
  • how a pretrained video diffusion transformer adapted to emit local trajectory-action chunks from egocentric history, proprioception and language.
  • the trick future frames act as dense consequence supervision during training only; at deployment the model decodes actions without generating video — the two-speed logic inside one net.
  • data a DiffAero-based pipeline with complementary Isaac-Lab and 3DGS renderers; a low-cost handheld capture device recreates flight-like egocentric trajectories; a self-guidance pass improves temporal consistency across overlapping chunks.
  • bad chunk length must be tuned; the decode still dominates when the video branch is kept.
ImagineUAV
2606.01205 WAM plus a kinodynamic planner: the learned part imagines the goal, the classical part turns it into a feasible track. Consistent long-horizon gains; two interfaces to tune.

Latent-side studies worth importing

entries
  • DiLA 2605.15725 — disentangled latent-action models from unlabelled video, asymmetric two-branch design.
  • Language-conditioned WMs 2511.22904 — dynamics-descriptive language, not merely task instructions, improves generalisation.
  • Predictability theory 2607.10362 — a control-theoretic account of prediction-error training for latent models inside planners.
  • Stability 2605.09241 subspace regularisation; belief space 2605.25313; V-JEPA 2 2506.09985 long-horizon with short-distance embeddings; FF-JEPA 2606.09311 latent planners.
  • CAPE 2606.07304 — contrastive action-conditioned parallel encoding; notes that small-but-critical action differences are harder to separate in latent space.

7 · Gaussian representations, precursors, the SLAM stack

Splat-Nav

2403.02751
what
A real-time pipeline in two modules over a Gaussian field.
how
  • Splat-Plan — a safe-by-construction polytope corridor from rigorous collision constraints, then a Bézier trajectory through it.
  • Splat-Loc — recursive pose estimates from the onboard RGB feed alone, exploiting the point-cloud form the field already has.
  • goals — by coordinates, or by language over a semantic field.
results
126 hardware flights; safety and speed equivalent to motion capture and visual odometry without their manual frame alignment; re-planning above 2 Hz, pose about 25 Hz — an order faster than the NeRF-based pipeline.
good
  • corridors make safety provable
  • the rate gap versus the older method is decisive
bad
  • a fitted map per site
  • corridor tightness must be tuned

SOUS VIDE / FiGS

2412.16346
what
A simulator, a training approach and a policy architecture together, for end-to-end visual navigation with zero-shot transfer on onboard sensing and compute only.
simulator
FiGS couples a computationally simple dynamics model to a high-fidelity Gaussian-Splatting reconstruction; up to 130 fps photorealistic images.
training
100k–300k image / state / action pairs from an expert MPC with privileged state and dynamics, randomised over dynamics parameters and spatial disturbances, distilled into a lightweight net.
policy
SV-Net consumes colour image, optical flow and IMU, emitting thrust and body-rate commands at 20 Hz; a learned module adapts at runtime to variations in the craft's own dynamics.
results
105 hardware experiments; robust to 30% mass change, 40 m/s gusts, 60% brightness change, moved or removed objects, and people crossing the field.
good
  • the best-documented zero-shot recipe
  • the three streams match the three quantities the inner loop needs
  • the perturbation list is a ready-made protocol
bad
  • a fitted scene per site
  • optical flow depends on the frame rate

ActiveGS and VISTA — the active-perception pair

ActiveGS
2412.17769 active scene reconstruction: which next view best reduces the field's uncertainty; published in a letters venue, tested on an unmanned platform. Directly reusable as a planner's next-best-view list; coverage stays limited in narrow passages.
VISTA
  • what 2507.01125 — open-vocabulary, task-relevant exploration with an online semantic field; a search instruction in, an informative trajectory out.
  • metric — a viewpoint-semantic coverage score quantifying geometric view diversity and task relevance in one number.
  • planning — receding-horizon trajectories prioritising semantic similarity to the query plus unexplored regions.
  • results — beats FisherRF and Bayes' Rays in computation speed and reconstruction quality on static sets; 6× higher success in challenging maps on a craft, matching them in easy ones; platform-agnostic across a quadrotor and a legged robot.
  • bad — the coverage weighting must be tuned; semantic quality caps the score.

Gaussian world models — the manipulation line

entries
  • GWM 2508.17600 — scalable Gaussian world models for manipulation.
  • GAF 2506.14135 — the Gaussian Action Field, a 4D form for dynamic scenes in manipulation.
  • GEM-4D 2605.22882 — geometry-enhanced video world model on LiDAR point clouds, asymmetric-resolution design; +26.8% sim, +34.3% real over the video-only baseline.
  • ManiGaussian 2403.08321 and the hierarchical bi-manual ++ 2506.19842.
caveat
all manipulation or ground: the primitive list is the natural interface, but none is tested on a flying craft.

The three precursors of the combined project

Gaussian-JEPA
2608.15651
  • predicts the representations of held-out primitive blocks from the visible context
  • an online encoder plus a stop-gradient target encoder, an exponential moving average of it, giving multi-scale targets; complementary target projections; feature-space grounding
  • no input-space decoder is needed at all
  • evaluated under resampling, partial observation, renderable shape completion, part segmentation, classification — better consistency and instance retention
  • it is representation pretraining: no language component, no navigation evaluation
4DGS-WAM
2608.25956
  • an object-centric model over the time-indexed field, separating movers from the static background
  • a policy model predicts the actors' future actions; a world model predicts the primitives' transformations; static content is reused from past frames, so extrapolation concentrates on the movers
  • on a driving set, with an ablation for the static-reuse choice
  • Gaussian-based world-action model, but driving, not aerial
GST-VLA
2603.09079
  • a Gaussian Spatial Tokenizer turns frozen dense depth and frozen semantic patch features into 128 anisotropic primitives — metric residual mean, log-scale covariance, learned opacity — pooled to a fixed budget
  • a 3D depth-aware chain-of-thought supervises four structured intermediate thoughts: object grounding, grasp-contact geometry, pairwise metric distances, coarse SE(3) waypoints
  • a 300M flow-matching action expert with a mixture-of-experts feedforward block decodes 7-DoF delta actions by conditional ODE integration
  • results: 96.4% on one manipulation suite (+2.0), 80.2% on the other (+5.4)
  • the primitive set is already the interface here — but manipulation, not a flying craft

Compression and uncertainty — ready-made blocks

entries
  • survey 2407.09510 — the compression landscape in one place.
  • LightGaussian 2311.17245 — 15× smaller at 200+ fps.
  • PUP 2406.10219 — principled uncertainty-guided pruning of redundant primitives.
  • PRIMU 2508.02443 — post-hoc primitive-level error and coverage straight from the primitives; the natural gate variable.
  • grouping 2312.00732 — primitives into instance-level groups for downstream tasks.
note
a budget of a few hundred primitives is realistic; that budget is the prefill token budget.

9 · Nesting and the two-speed design

The ingredients, with their exact contribution

nesting
  • MRL 2205.13147 — one run, many nested sub-models, each beating a from-scratch model of its size; the prefix is the small piece.
  • MatFormer 2310.07707 — nested transformer for elastic inference; many sub-settings from one training.
  • M3 2405.17430 — the same for the multimodal case, where vision tokens dominate the budget.
depth
a halting unit sets the depth per sample 2004.13542; per-token recurrence converges to the same state 2607.14427 — a different mechanism from the fixed prefix.
two speeds
shared-weight fast-and-slow 2405.17418; asynchronous branches 2512.20188; dual-rate on-device 2605.00884 — the three realisations of the same idea.
forgetting
zero on twelve suites after action tuning 2509.22195; insulating the base helps yet is not a guaranteed cure; in-context updates ease the plasticity loss 2605.12484. Report both scores side by side.

11 · The eight challenges, goods and bads

1 · Latency and onboard compute

Good — two working strategies: hierarchical splitting, the two-speed design, nearly universal; and staleness tolerance, fused asynchronous branches with a six-second tolerance noted. Hardware-optimised lines reach 6.5× and 8.66×.

Bad — capability papers print no timing; no reporting norm; mass and power rarely stated; no incident log, so every safety claim is architectural.

2 · Generalisation, the sim-to-real gap

Good — named by both surveys; measurable with mixed protocols; zero-shot transfer shown three times over.

Bad — map removal 46.8%→22.5%; 87→31 on the leading model; only a few methods ever flew; no controlled paired study found.

3 · Safety and formal guarantees

Good — the whole toolbox above is cheap on the small pieces.

Bad — still future work in the roadmap; hallucination persists; unapplied to aerial policies.

4 · Data and metrics

Good — real scanned cities; generous sizes; a standard five-way split.

Bad — radius drift 1–5 m and 20 versus 15 m; thin indoor coverage; varying instruction styles.

5 · Multi-drone

Good — early attempts exist; the leader–follower pairing is natural.

Bad — unsettled whether gains come from the structure or merely from capacity; overhead and staleness.

6 · Long-horizon memory, the SLAM seam

Good — a mature factor-graph stack maps straight onto the need; the primitive map is already an explicit queryable memory.

Bad — the reviewed VLA papers rarely expose a factor-graph memory interface; map age, pose uncertainty, and viewpoint change remain poorly measured.

7 · Regulation

Good — a written pathway: the June 2025 order and the proposed Part 108 with automated detection and avoidance, remote identification and approved service providers.

Bad — no reviewed technical paper ties its design to those clauses.

8 · The Gaussian gap

Good — mature simulator, compression and uncertainty; three precursors already cover the parts.

Bad — no paper located here demonstrates the complete combination of predicted Gaussian/geometric state, language-conditioned 6-DoF action selection, calibrated uncertainty, delay-aware execution, and controlled real-flight transfer with measured onboard latency.

14 · Supervisor-facing novelty audit

Bottom line

not new
Language-conditioned UAV control, Gaussian-splat flight simulation, Gaussian-based real-to-sim transfer, JEPA-style latent planning, uncertainty-aware candidate scoring, and two-speed latency handling have all appeared in close prior work.
still open
No paper located here demonstrates the complete combination on a real UAV: action-sensitive geometric prediction, calibrated uncertainty that changes behavior, delay expressed in flight distance, and controlled transfer to held-out real routes.
claim to use
The contribution should be a measurable mechanism for action-sensitive 6-DoF prediction and safe execution—not “the first JEPA UAV,” “the first Gaussian VLA,” or “the first language-conditioned drone.”

Closest prior work: contribution, strength, limitation, opening

UAV-Flow
NeurIPS 2025. Real-world language-conditioned fine-grained UAV control benchmark. Strength: concrete aerial task and expert trajectories. Limit: benchmark, not a new predictive mechanism. Open: use it to test stale observations, dynamic obstacles, and safety—not to claim language-to-action novelty.
SINGER
UAV generalist navigation. Language-conditioned onboard policy with a 3DGS simulator and hardware transfer. Strength: end-to-end system evidence. Limit: does not establish action-sensitive calibrated latent prediction. Open: show a causal benefit from geometric prediction under partial maps, fast motion, and delayed sensing.
GRAD-NAV++
RA-L 2026. Onboard aerial VLA with photorealistic 3DGS simulation and real-flight evaluation. Strength: very close system prior. Limit: differentiable simulation is not the same as calibrated future imagination. Open: isolate whether an action-conditioned geometric state reduces failures.
Splat-Nav / SOUS VIDE
T-RO / RA-L lines. Gaussian maps and Gaussian-splat simulators already support safe navigation and sim-to-real flight. Strength: real hardware and disturbance testing. Limit: not language-conditioned JEPA planning. Open: add a predictive state or uncertainty-to-action mechanism, not Gaussian rendering alone.
PiJEPA
CVPR Workshop 2026. Policy-guided JEPA-style planning for language-conditioned navigation. Strength: direct precedent for language plus latent planning. Limit: not high-speed 6-DoF UAV control. Open: add flight geometry, braking/recoverability, observation age, and hardware timing.
Calibrated Predictive Safety
2026 preprint. Action chunks, action-conditioned JEPA, calibrated risk/progress, deterministic safety shield, fallback ladder. Strength: closest prior to selective latent planning. Limit: simulation-only and not UAV-specific. Open: a genuinely new speed-aware recoverability mechanism with real-flight evidence.
Think Like a Pilot
2026 UAV preprint. Slow semantic pilot, fast action model, and delayed-feature alignment. Strength: directly addresses action staleness. Limit: fast inference does not guarantee correct or safe actions. Open: learn a validity boundary tied to speed, braking distance, and predicted recoverability.
VLM-MPPI
September 2026 UAV preprint. Asynchronous VLM selection among six behavior-conditioned MPPI trajectory modes; MPPI replans at 20 Hz and the paper reports real-quadrotor tests. Strength: strong practical latency baseline. Limit: no learned latent world model or calibrated uncertainty; 20 Hz is not a high-speed safety proof. Open: predict candidate validity over delay and trigger slowdown, re-observation, or fallback.
EmbodiedSplat / Atlas
ICCV 2025 / vendor system. Personalized real-to-sim navigation and camera-conditioned scene reconstruction. Strength: target-environment simulation is practical and relevant. Limit: not UAV physics; Atlas is not independently validated here. Open: held-out UAV transfer, metric geometry validation, moving obstacles, and reduced real-data requirements.

15 · Architecture audit: what “using a VLM/VLA” actually means

Model labels are not comparable architectures

VLM-as-perception
The model outputs text, detections, semantic labels, depth, or a structured scene description. A separate planner/controller flies. Inspectable and easier to keep outside the safety loop, but the semantic bottleneck may discard metric geometry.
VLM selector
The model chooses a waypoint or candidate trajectory while MPPI/MPC/PID handles high-rate control. Better latency structure, but the candidate set may omit the correct behavior and the selection can become stale.
End-to-end VLA
The model outputs velocity, action tokens, thrust/angular velocity, or action chunks. Direct and task-oriented, but opaque, data-hungry, hard to calibrate, and dependent on the action space matching aircraft dynamics.
World/JEPA
The model predicts a future latent/state under an action and a planner scores candidates. Efficient compared with video generation, but only useful if the latent preserves controllable geometry and uncertainty.
3DGS backend
The representation may be a map, renderer, memory, policy input, predicted state, or simulator. “Uses Gaussian splats” is incomplete until that role is specified.

Implementation-level comparisons

LookasideVLN
CVPR 2026. Adds an Egocentric Lookaside Graph for directional landmark relations, a Spatial Landmark Knowledge Base for lightweight retrieval, and a Lookaside MLLM agent. It also uses situation-specific prompts and visited-landmark history summaries. Meaning: its novelty is a memory/reference-frame paradigm, not simply an MLLM prompt.
UAV-Flow
NeurIPS 2025. Adapts recurrent VLN and VLA architectures to atomic language-conditioned fine-grained flight, with real/sim trajectories and an OpenVLA-UAV baseline. Meaning: “VLM/VLA baseline” must specify action horizon, history, action representation, control rate, and aerial adaptation.
SINGER
Onboard policy. Semantics-rich 3DGS simulator + RRT*-style trajectories + MPC expert + lightweight imitation policy mapping RGB and a language goal directly to control. Meaning: the online policy is not a large VLM; semantic knowledge is distilled into a compact closed-loop controller.
GRAD-NAV++
RA-L 2026. Differentiable 3DGS simulation, DiffRL, and a mixture-of-experts action head for compact onboard visual-language-action control. Meaning: one-model language-to-flight action generation is already a concrete prior.
VLM-MPPI
September 2026 preprint. Six behaviour-conditioned MPPI planners generate intentionally different feasible trajectories; an asynchronous VLM selects the candidate from an overlaid FPV image; MPPI replans at 20 Hz and PID tracks it. Meaning: a new fast VLM selector alone is not novel.
AutoFly
ICLR 2026. End-to-end VLA with pseudo-depth features and progressive visual/depth/language/action alignment for unknown outdoor navigation. Meaning: an explicit geometric latent must beat a strong pseudo-depth/action baseline under partial and delayed sensing.
PiJEPA
CVPR Workshop 2026. Policy prior + frozen visual encoder + action-conditioned JEPA prediction + MPPI-style planning. Meaning: “language-conditioned JEPA planning” is already a close prior; UAV novelty must be 6-DoF feasibility, recoverability, timing, and hardware evidence.
Calibrated Predictive Safety
2026 preprint. Action proposer + action-conditioned JEPA + calibrated risk/progress heads + deterministic safety shield and fallback. Meaning: generic uncertainty-aware latent candidate filtering is already covered.
Gaussian-JEPA / 4DGS-WAM / GST-VLA
Representation precedents. Predictive Gaussian tokens, object-centric Gaussian dynamics, and Gaussian spatial-token interfaces already exist. Meaning: state explicitly whether Gaussians are used for mapping, rendering, memory, policy input, predicted future, uncertainty, or action decoding.

What would actually differ in our system?

input
RGB-D/stereo, VIO/SLAM state, map age, pose uncertainty, velocity, and instruction.
language
Convert instruction into a structured subgoal and constraints rather than repeating a free-form prompt at every control step.
prediction
Condition a compact geometric latent predictor on candidate action chunks and predict future clearance, relative pose, progress, and validity.
execution
Use MPPI or another feasible local planner, a hard collision/recoverability check, and an uncertainty-triggered slow-down, re-observation, or fallback.
evidence
Hold the controller and candidate planner constant across VLM-as-perception, VLM-MPPI selector, end-to-end VLA, predictive-only JEPA, action-conditioned JEPA, and full uncertainty/fallback variants.

Non-novel “VLM on top” changes

insufficient
Replacing one VLM with Qwen; prompting left/right/forward; adding a language embedding to an existing geometric policy; using a VLM to select a waypoint from a Gaussian map; swapping in a VLA action head; increasing planner frequency; or adding Atlas images without a transfer result.
test
The architecture must reduce a specific failure—action confusion, stale semantic decisions, overconfident latent rollouts, or sim-to-real failure—under matched latency and real-flight conditions.
